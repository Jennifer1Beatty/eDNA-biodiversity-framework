Metabarcoding

Utilized for broad biodiversity assessments across multiple trophic levels simultaneously using high-throughput sequencing panels. Metabarcoding yields taxonomic composition of the community but does not provide absolute species abundance. It involves extracting the DNA from the environment and amplifying DNA regions targeting specific taxonomic groups using PCR.

Pros:

  • Non-invasive
  • Relatively inexpensive

Cons:

  • Low taxonomic specificity of primers
  • Incomplete reference database
  • DNA degradation and dispersal
  • Abundance estimation of pluricellular organisms
  • Lack of Standardization
  • Cannot be used for abundance

ddPCR / qPCR

Best for targeted species monitoring (e.g., invasive pests or highly endangered target species) where quantitative sensitivity is paramount. Quantitative PCR (qPCR) and digital droplet PCR (ddPCR) yields information on the presence and abundance of specific targeted taxa or species. It involves extracting DNA from the environment and amplifying DNA regions specific to highly specified taxonomic groups using qPCR and ddPCR machines.

Pros:

  • Non-invasive
  • Generates abundances of organisms

Cons:

  • Limited species specific primers
  • Incomplete reference database

Metagenomics

Shotgun sequencing applications used to capture whole environmental genomes without targeted PCR bias. Metagenomics yields taxonomic identification and potential function of organisms present. It involves extracting DNA from the environment and sequencing the DNA without targeting with specific primers or amplifying targeted regions using PCR.

Pros:

  • Non-invasive
  • PCR independent approach

Cons:

  • Computational complex

Sample collection

Importance elements to consider are:

  • Depth of samples in water or soil
  • Volume of material processed
  • Processing time of material
  • Filter choice
  • Preservation of samples

Metadata should include at the very least:

  • Location (Latitude, Longitude) of sampling
  • Date and time of sampling
  • Depth of sampling
  • Volume of material processed
  • Environmental conditions (ie. temperature)

PCR Primers

qPCR/ddPCR primers are more targeted and need to be carefully designed to ensure accuracy.

Metabarcoding primers target entire groups: Fish (COI, 16S, 12S), plankton (18S- microbial eukaryotes; 16S- prokaryotes), fungi, virus, mammal, birds.

Metabarcoding targets:

  • Bacteria- 16S
  • Eukaryotes- 18S and/or 28S
  • Metazoan- COI

Bioinformatics Pipeline

Standard data processing flows including quality filtering, denoising (ASVs vs. OTUs), chimera checks, and taxonomy assignment. Bioinformatics pipeline refers to all the steps taken from raw sequences obtained from the sequencing platform, to the dataset used for biodiversity measurements. Standard algorithms are available for quality control, trimming, and chimera checking. Decisions must be made as to how sequences are compared to reference databases to assign taxonomy.
